# WORLD-023C next integration loop — prioritized changes

1. **Shared stewardship/maintenance agreements (P0):** introduce explicit funding commitments, partner backup, coownership and consequences for broken promises. Do not automatically rescue failing worlds; test interventions against an autonomous no-agreement control.
2. **World ecology (P0):** implement decomposer and pollinator *agents* and a real nutrient feedback, then fit life stages, fertility, carrying capacity and climate sensitivities against defensible ecological patterns. Test 200+ seasons across at least 30 matched seeds.
3. **Physical seed compiler and transaction atomicity (P0):** version and fingerprint approved blueprints, ensure preflight/commit/rollback across ledgers and actor registries, and establish a Sea substrate stock or explicitly defined energy-to-matter mechanism.
4. **Cognition and learning (P1):** connect a knowledge prerequisite graph to seed development, encode incomplete explanations, cue-dependent forgetting and source confidence; prevent full mastery after a few observations.
5. **World residents and organizations (P1):** introduce species-specialized actors with variable goals and access, mortal social relationships, learning and local institutions, without permitting House travel.
6. **Underworld Flowers (P1):** design an event-driven death-routing interface; keep souls fictional and material/energy ledgers physically audited.
7. **UI/observatory (P1):** show causal chains from world reports → Founder decisions → energy transactions → population effects, plus stewardship contracts, event replay, and an interior world map.

**Recommended experiment:** ST-01 cooperative stewardship, four cloned worlds per seed (single owner, close-partner backup, research-council governance, competing factions), with same climates, initial ecological stocks and budgets. Outcomes: tree persistence, interruption frequency, material/energy accounting, trust update after broken promises, founder effort distribution, population dynamics. Compare results against scripted maintenance and zero-maintenance controls; require source-linked event traces and preregistered failure criteria.
